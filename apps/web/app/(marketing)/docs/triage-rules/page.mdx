# Triage rules

Triage is what turns a pile of finished calls into a short list of things a person
needs to do. It runs on every call, and its decision is derived from typed
fields — never from reading the summary text.

That distinction is the guarantee. A decision derived from prose cannot be audited; a
decision derived from a boolean can.

## The rules, in order

The first matching signal wins, so the order matters.

| Signal | Disposition |
|---|---|
| Asked never to be called again | **Needs a person** — and the number is suppressed |
| Explicitly asked for a person | **Needs a person** |
| Frustration detected | **Needs a person** |
| The agent judged the conversation reached no clear resolution | **Needs a person** |
| Negative tone, no frustration | **Queued for retry** |
| Busy, no answer, or voicemail | **Queued for retry** |
| Failed or cancelled | **Unreachable** |
| Completed, but a required field was never answered | **Needs a person**, with the missing fields named |
| Completed with no escalation signal | **Auto-closed** |
| Anything else | **Skipped**, with the status named |

Two of those deserve their own explanation.

## The agent's own verdict on the conversation

Separately from whatever the fields say, the agent reports whether the conversation
actually reached a clear resolution. An explicit "no" there is an escalation.

It sits below the three signals above it because those are more specific and more
actionable — somebody who asked for a human has told you exactly what to do next. It
sits above the plain-status buckets below it because a considered "this did not
resolve" is a real signal, not the absence of one.

## A required field that never got answered

A call that connected, ran to completion, and still ended without a field you marked
**required** is escalated rather than auto-closed. The reason names the fields, in
words, so the queue reads as an instruction:

```text
The call ended without party size and travel date - a person needs to ask.
```

That is the point of marking a field required: not to make the agent invent a value,
but to stop a call missing something that matters from being counted as closed. See
[Result schemas](/docs/result-schemas).

Note where the rule sits — **last**, below every retry and unreachable bucket, and it
only applies to a call whose status is `completed`. That placement is deliberate. A
busy signal, a voicemail, and a failed call all ended without your required fields too,
and of course they did: nobody was ever asked. Escalating those for "missing fields"
would replace a useful "worth retrying" signal with a useless one. Only a call that
genuinely had the chance to answer and did not is a call a person needs to chase.

## A bad time is not a bad mood

This is the rule that earns the product its keep, and it is worth stating on its own.

Negative tone with no frustration is *not* an escalation. Someone who says "I'm
driving, can you call back?" sounds negative to any sentiment model and is not
unhappy with you — they are unavailable. Escalating them wastes a person's afternoon
and loses nothing to a polite retry instead.

Frustration is the separate signal, and it is the one that reaches a human.

## What the lamps mean

| Lamp | Disposition |
|---|---|
| dim | Queued, not yet dialled |
| brass | Ringing or in conversation |
| brass, pulsing | Queued for retry |
| jade | Auto-closed — a clean outcome |
| flare | Needs a person |
| ice | Reserved, currently unassigned |

`ice` used to mean a simulated call. Dry run was removed, so the colour is held back
rather than reassigned — a lamp colour that has meant two different things is worse
than one that means nothing yet.

Every lamp in the interface is paired with a text label. Colour is never the only
carrier of the meaning.

## Opt-outs are permanent and global

An opt-out does two things at once: the call ends, and the number goes onto your
suppression list.

The suppression list applies to **every agent and every run in your organisation,
forever**. It is not per-agent, it cannot be overridden from a run, and re-importing a
CSV that contains a suppressed number does not bring it back. A suppressed number is
skipped before a call is placed.

## Reading the decision on a call

Open any call and the triage decision is shown as a short chain:

```text
Frustration detected → Needs a person
```

Each step names the typed field it read. If you disagree with a decision, the
transcript is one click away and the field that drove it is on screen — so a
disagreement is a two-second check rather than an argument.

## Escalations are a worklist, not a report

The **Needs a person** queue sorts oldest first, deliberately. The oldest escalation
is the most expensive one: someone was frustrated, nobody has called them back, and
every hour makes that worse.

Each item carries the reason, the transcript excerpt around the trigger, and three
actions — call back yourself, reassign, or mark resolved.
